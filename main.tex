The gate-voltage transition region (TR) that separates the subthreshold and above-threshold regimes is a critical bottleneck to supply-voltage ($\mathbf{V_{\mathrm{DD}}}$) scaling of field-effect transistors. This constraint is particularly severe in amorphous oxide semiconductor FETs (a-OSFETs), where high densities of shallow traps induce a wide TR width ($\mathbf{V_{\mathrm{TR}}}$). In this work, we investigate the physical origins of the TR and introduce a robust extraction scheme to quantify $\mathbf{V_{\mathrm{TR}}}$, because it is an essential figure of merit alongside subthreshold swing and mobility. By identifying and systematically suppressing specific sources of shallow traps, we establish process and device design guidelines to minimize $\mathbf{V_{\mathrm{TR}}}$. Specifically, we demonstrate in experimental results that $\mathbf{V_{\mathrm{TR}}}$ can be reduced by (a) increasing the indium concentration, (b) increasing the channel thickness, (c) employing gate dielectrics with superior interface quality, and (d) passivating shallow oxygen vacancies by oxygen annealing. Furthermore, we demonstrate $\mathbf{1.72\times}$ reduction in $\mathbf{V_{\mathrm{TR}}}$ using a double-gated a-OSFET compared to its bottom-gated counterpart, confirming that enhanced electrostatic gate control is a highly effective mitigation strategy. This study establishes that minimizing $\mathbf{V_{\mathrm{TR}}}$ is a fundamental prerequisite for deploying a-OSFETs in advanced technology nodes with $\mathbf{V_{\mathrm{DD}} < 0.7}$ V and provides actionable pathways to achieve this goal.

\end{abstract}
